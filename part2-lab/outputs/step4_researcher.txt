Overfitting refers to a situation where a machine learning model learns the training data too well, capturing its noise and idiosyncrasies rather than the 
underlying pattern. This results in poor generalization to new, unseen data. The bias-variance tradeoff is a fundamental concept that shows the error of a 
model is a sum of bias (simplicity of the model), variance (model's sensitivity to the training data), and irreducible error (noise in the data). To avoid 
overfitting, we often increase model complexity (lower bias, but potentially higher variance), use regularization techniques, or collect more diverse 
training data (reduce variance). Striking the right balance between bias and variance is crucial for building effective models.
