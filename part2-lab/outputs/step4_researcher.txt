Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too closely, resulting in poor generalization to new, unseen data. This happens when 
the model becomes excessively complex, capturing noise and idiosyncrasies in the training data instead of the underlying patterns. The 
bias-variance tradeoff refers to the balance between bias (simplicity of the model, leading to higher bias and lower variance) and variance 
(complexity of the model, leading to lower bias and higher variance). A model with low bias and high variance is prone to overfitting, while a 
model with high bias and low variance risks underfitting. Striking the right balance between bias and variance is crucial for effective machine 
learning.
