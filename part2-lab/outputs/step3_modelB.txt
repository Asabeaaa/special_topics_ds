Overfitting in machine learning is akin to a student who memorizes every detail of a textbook rather than understanding its concepts. This student can 
answer any question about the textbook precisely but fails to answer new questions or apply the knowledge to real-world problems. In machine learning, 
overfitting occurs when a model learns the noise in the training data instead of the underlying patterns, resulting in poor generalization to new, unseen 
data.

To reduce overfitting, one common approach is to use Regularization, which adds a penalty term to the loss function to discourage complex models from 
learning the noise in the training data. This is like a teacher giving the student a difficult assignment that requires understanding the concepts rather 
than memorization, thereby improving the student's ability to solve new problems.
