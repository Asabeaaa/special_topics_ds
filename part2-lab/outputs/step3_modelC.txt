In machine learning, overfitting occurs when a model learns the detail and noise in the training data to the extent that it negatively impacts the 
performance on new data. Imagine you're trying to predict the weather using a model that's so good at remembering every weather pattern you've ever 
experienced that it starts to predict the weather for your shower. The model can be perfect at remembering past showers but poorly at predicting when 
you'll actually shower. To reduce overfitting, you should use techniques like cross-validation, early stopping, or simplifying the model's complexity to 
ensure it generalizes well to unseen data.
